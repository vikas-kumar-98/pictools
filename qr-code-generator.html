What Is a QR Code?

A QR code is a machine-readable optical label that contains data about the item to which it is attached. It consists of black squares arranged in a square grid on a white background, which can be read by an imaging device like a smartphone camera.

QR codes can store various types of information:

  • Website URLs — Direct users to your site
  • Contact Details — vCard or meCard data
  • Wi-Fi Credentials — Connect devices instantly
  • Email Addresses — Pre-fill email messages
  • Phone Numbers — Call or SMS
  • Plain Text — Any short message

Static vs Dynamic QR Codes

📦 Static QR Codes

Contain fixed, unchangeable data. Once generated, the content cannot be modified. Perfect for permanent information like business cards or product labels.

🔄 Dynamic QR Codes

Redirect to a short URL that can be changed anytime. Ideal for marketing campaigns, A/B testing, and situations where content may need updating.

Dynamic QR codes offer more flexibility and analytics but require a service to manage the redirection. Static codes are simpler and don't rely on external services.

QR Code Best Practices

  • Ensure high contrast between foreground and background
  • Test your QR code on multiple devices before printing
  • Provide a call-to-action near the QR code
  • Use high-resolution images for print applications
  • Keep sufficient white space (quiet zone) around the code
  • Consider dynamic QR codes for marketing campaigns
  • Use error correction for codes that may be damaged
  • Monitor QR code performance with analytics
